- Device pixel ratio capped at 2. Handle `resize` so it fits any viewport from 360 by 360 up to full screen. Readable at 840 by 540.
- It must do something visible within two seconds, run forever, and restart itself when it ends. No "click to start".

- Do not handle the key `r` or `R`; the Labs page uses it. Avoid keyboard shortcuts in general; mouse and touch are fine where the spec says so, and everything must still run with no input at all.
- Keep each file under about 45 KB. Comment sparingly and only where the algorithm needs it.

## Experiments

### city, "Code city" (Three.js)
Data: `/previews/code-city.json`, an array of `{ "path": "src/pages/HomePage.tsx", "size": 14572, "ext": "tsx", "dir": "src/pages" }` for this repository, generated at build time. Lay the files out as a city: one district per top-level directory (src, public, supabase, docs, scripts, .github, root files), districts packed in a grid with streets between them, each file a box whose footprint is fixed and whose height is `log2(size)` scaled, colour by extension (ts and tsx bone `#f1ece3` at 70%, css copper, html and json muted, sql and md darker). Ground plane dark, a faint fog. Camera orbits slowly on its own; pointer drag orbits; wheel zooms within limits. Hover a box: it brightens and the HUD shows `path` and size in KB. HUD: `Files <n>`, `Districts <n>`, `Largest <path>`. Eyebrow `Preview · Code city`. Tagline `This site, as a city.` Bottom caption `Built from the repository at deploy time`.

### system, "Exploded system" (Three.js)
The client portal taken apart as an exploded diagram, five parts stacked on a vertical axis with gaps, each a flat slab with a text label drawn on a canvas texture: `Browser` (React single-page app, static hosting), `Edge functions` (privileged actions run here, never in the browser), `Database` (Postgres with row-level security, each client sees only their own rows), `Storage` (files, access checked per client), `Email` (sent when a pack or invoice is ready). Thin lines join the parts in order. Auto-orbit slowly; drag to orbit. Every four seconds the next part lifts a little and turns copper while the HUD shows its one-line description; hover or tap a part to select it instead. HUD: the selected part's name and sentence. Eyebrow `Preview · Exploded system`. Tagline `The client portal, taken apart.` Bottom caption `Browser · edge functions · database · storage · email`.

### portrait, "Portrait in particles" (Three.js)
Load `/images/john-byrne.png` (400 by 400, black and white) with an Image and a hidden canvas; sample it to about six thousand points on a grid, skipping the darkest pixels; each point's z comes from brightness (bright forward, up to about 12 percent of the width) and its colour from the pixel value tinted towards bone. Render as `THREE.Points` with a small round sprite (draw the sprite on a canvas, radial fade). At rest the cloud breathes slowly and the camera drifts a few degrees. Pointer movement pushes points away within a radius of the cursor (project the cursor onto the portrait plane) and they ease back over about two seconds. Touch does the same. HUD: `Points <n>`. Eyebrow `Preview · Portrait in particles`. Tagline `A few thousand points, one person.` Bottom caption `Move the mouse through it`.

### flock, "Migration" (Three.js)
Three hundred boids in 3D with separation, alignment and cohesion, a soft bound that keeps them in a wide box, and a slow wind that carries the flock from left to right and back again over about forty seconds. A year passes every forty seconds: the clear colour and a single directional light move through winter (cold, dim), spring, summer (warm, bright), autumn (copper), and the HUD names the season. Birds are small elongated triangles (a BufferGeometry instanced or merged) pointing along their velocity. Camera orbits slowly; drag to orbit. HUD: `Birds 300`, `Season <name>`. Eyebrow `Preview · Migration`. Tagline `Three hundred birds, one rule each.` Bottom caption `Separation · alignment · cohesion · wind`.

### snake-learns, "Snake learns" (2D canvas)
Tabular Q-learning, nothing given to it except the reward: plus for food, minus for dying, a small minus per step. State: danger straight, left, right (three booleans), current direction (four), food direction relative to the head (dx sign, dy sign). Actions: straight, turn left, turn right. Epsilon decays from 1.0 to 0.02 over the first few hundred episodes, alpha 0.1, gamma 0.9. Run many steps per animation frame (about 120, adjust so it visibly improves inside a minute on a laptop) but draw only the current episode's snake on a 20 by 20 board at the left; at the right draw the learning curve, score per episode as a thin line with a 50-episode moving average in copper. HUD: `Episode <n>`, `Score <n>`, `Best <n>`, `Epsilon <0.00>`. Eyebrow `Preview · Snake learns`. Tagline `It starts stupid.` Bottom caption `Q-learning · no rules given · never restarts`.

### cars, "Cars learn to drive" (2D canvas)
Neuro-evolution. A closed track drawn from a smooth loop (two offset curves make the walls), forty cars at the start line, each with five distance sensors (rays at 0, plus and minus 30, plus and minus 70 degrees) feeding a tiny network (5 inputs, 6 hidden, 2 outputs: steer and throttle) with random weights in generation one. A car dies when it touches a wall. A generation ends when every car is dead or after twenty seconds; fitness is distance travelled along the track centre line; the top eight breed the next forty by copying and mutating weights (small gaussian noise, a few large). Draw dead cars faint, living cars bone, the current best copper with its sensors drawn. HUD: `Generation <n>`, `Alive <n>`, `Best lap <0.00 laps>`. Eyebrow `Preview · Cars learn to drive`. Tagline `Most crash. The best breed.` Bottom caption `Five sensors · a tiny network · evolution`.

### teach, "Teach it a shape" (2D canvas, interactive)
The visitor draws a shape with the mouse or finger on the canvas, then presses one of three label buttons below it: `Circle`, `Square`, `Zigzag` (buttons are plain, bone text on a hairline, copper when active; pointer events are on). Each stroke is resampled to 32 points, scaled and rotated to a canonical form (the $1 unistroke recogniser), and stored under its label. From the moment a class has one example, every new stroke is matched by nearest neighbour and the guess is shown in the HUD with its score before the visitor labels it. Until the visitor touches the canvas, a demo runs: the page draws its own wobbly circle, square and zigzag one at a time, labels them, then draws new ones and shows its guesses, so the idea is visible with no input; the first touch stops the demo and clears nothing. HUD: `Examples <n>`, `Guess <label or none>`, `Score <0.00>`. Eyebrow `Preview · Teach it a shape`. Tagline `Show it three times. Then it knows.` Bottom caption `Draw, label, draw again`.

### balance, "Keep it balanced" (2D canvas, interactive)
A cart on a rail with a pole balanced on it, real physics (cart-pole equations, gravity, friction) stepped at a fixed timestep, and a PID controller driving the cart to keep the pole upright and the cart near the centre. Three small sliders in the bottom area labelled `P`, `I`, `D` (pointer events on, bone track, copper thumb) let the visitor detune it until it oscillates or falls; it recovers or restarts automatically. Clicking or tapping on the stage pushes the pole from that side; every six seconds without input the page nudges it itself. A strip chart at the top right draws the pole angle over the last ten seconds, with a copper zero line. HUD: `Angle <0.0 deg>`, `Push <n>`, `Falls <n>`. Eyebrow `Preview · Keep it balanced`. Tagline `A loop that keeps a thing standing.` Bottom caption `PID control · tap to push it · tune the gains`.

### eventloop, "The event loop" (2D, DOM or canvas)
A model of how the JavaScript runtime orders work, stepped slowly so it can be read: four boxes, `Call stack`, `Web APIs`, `Microtasks`, `Tasks`, and a console. Three short sample programs, chosen by plain buttons above the console: one with `setTimeout(0)` and `Promise.then`, one with a timer inside a microtask, one with a fetch-like callback. Each program is shown as code on the left with the current line highlighted; frames move between the boxes as blocks with their names at about one move per second; the console prints what the real engine would print in that order. Say on the surface that it is a model: bottom caption `A model of the runtime · stepped slowly · the order is the real order`. Verify the output order of each sample against Node before you commit to it. Loops through the three programs when no one chooses. HUD: `Program <name>`, `Step <n>`. Eyebrow `Preview · The event loop`. Tagline `Where the time goes.`

### migrate, "Spreadsheet to system" (2D canvas or DOM)
Left: a spreadsheet of demo rows (30 at a time), columns `Name`, `Email`, `Joined`, `Balance`, visibly messy: trailing spaces, three date formats, emails without an at sign, amounts with pound signs or commas, two duplicates, one row with a name in capitals. Right: the system, a clean table with a schema line above it: `customer · email · joined (ISO date) · balance (pence)`. Rows travel across one at a time, about one per second, through a short pipeline drawn between the two: `trim`, `parse date`, `parse money`, `validate email`, `dedupe`; a row that fails is turned copper and dropped into a `Rejected` list under the system with its reason; a row that was fixed on the way shows a small `fixed` mark. A log line scrolls at the bottom. When the batch is done the page waits three seconds and starts a new random batch. Demo data: generate names and emails from word lists, never real people. HUD: `Migrated <n>`, `Fixed <n>`, `Rejected <n>`. Eyebrow `Preview · Spreadsheet to system`. Tagline `Row by row, checked on the way.` Bottom caption `Demo data · every row checked · restarts`.

### cutover, "Switching the database under traffic" (2D canvas)
Two stores side by side, `Old` and `New`, each a box with a row count; a traffic source at the top emits requests as small dots at a steady rate, reads bone and writes copper, which travel to a store and vanish on arrival. Phases, each lasting about eight seconds, named in the HUD and logged with a timestamp on the right: 1 `Reads and writes to old`; 2 `Dual write` (writes go to both); 3 `Backfill` (a bar under New fills as its row count climbs to match); 4 `Shift reads` (the share of reads to New rises 10, 50, 90, 100 percent); 5 `Retire old` (Old dims, its count greys); then the log notes `done, no requests lost`, waits, and the loop restarts. Count every request that arrives; show `Lost 0` only if it is true in the simulation. HUD: `Phase <name>`, `Reads to new <n %>`, `Rows <old> / <new>`, `Lost 0`. Eyebrow `Preview · Switching the database under traffic`. Tagline `Nobody notices. That is the point.` Bottom caption `Dual write · backfill · shift reads · retire`.
